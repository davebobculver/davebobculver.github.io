Continuous and Differentiable Neural Network Representations of the Surfzone Sea-Surface from Lidar Point Clouds

March 2024 – September 2026

This project focused on applying a traditional neural network architecture to solving a novel oceanic sensing problem. Prior attempts of reconstructing sea surface lidar returns used traditional interpolation methods, and I adapted a methodology to return continuous and differentiable representations using sinusoidal representation networks (SIREN)

How it works

  • We train a neural network to regress the sea surface from a set of lidar data
  • We implement a so-called "sinusoidal representation network" (SIREN) creating an implicit neural representation or neural field of the sea surface
  • We required a modification of the principled SIREN initialization and used a standard Xavier normal initialization.

My contribution

  • I created the training pipeline, preprocessed the data, created the model architecture in custom PyTorch modules, evaluated the model, and documented the results.
  • In the documentation, I described the methodology, created figures, and led the work on the paper while collaborating with 2 professors who helped me along the research process.

Medical Image CNN Classification

December 2025

A group of classmates and I reproduced Eff-PCNet, an efficient CNN architecture for medical image classification, to test whether its claims hold up under tight compute limits. We built a lightweight version and benchmarked it against a standard transfer-learning baseline Eff-PCNet across three medical image datasets.

How it works

  • Our EffPCNet-Small splits channels across 3×3 depthwise, 1×K, K×1, and identity branches to approximate a large receptive field cheaply. This is the main claim from the original paper, which we reproduced in at a smaller scale.
  • We benchmarked it against a frozen EfficientNetV2-S transfer-learning baseline on HAM10000, SkinCancer MNIST, and Chest X-ray Pneumonia, scoring accuracy, F1, and AUC.
  • At a fifth of the baseline's parameters (4.39M vs. 20.3M), it still beat it on both skin-lesion datasets — SkinCancer accuracy rose from 0.64 to 0.85 — supporting the paper's core efficiency claim.

My contribution

  • I built the lightweight EffPCNet-Small architecture and implemented the benchmarking framework.
  • I managed the project timeline, coordinated team progress, and created a team presentation.

Automatic Waffler — Phys 122 Class Project

March 2026

The Automatic Waffler is better seen as a semi-automatic waffle making tool. It was created with an Amazon basic waffle iron, aluminum extrusion, plywood, custom 3d printed parts, a bench top power supple, and Arduino controlled electronics. The electronics consisted of 1 stepper motor (w/ motor shield), a solenoid valve, a power relay, a 10kg load cell, and a signal amplifier for the load cell. The load cell used Serial communication with my computer to sense how much better has been deposited, thus dispensing the perfect amount of batter for your waffle. (We won the class favorite prize!)

How it works

  • I wrote code in C++ to both communicate with load cell and to power each electrical component. We used the onboard USB type-b connection to communicate with the Arduino.
  • The architecture of the contraption was designed in SolidWorks, which we referenced during construction. The 3d printed parts where also made in SolidWorks.

My contribution

  • I had one partner on this project, and I was the main source of inspiration. I made every part, wrote every line of code, and came up with each idea. My partner was a great asset during the documentation and construction, but the project was run by me.
  • We only had a limited amount of electronics and resources, but I stretched out what we had and made the final project run in time for the presentation!

  • SolidWorks
